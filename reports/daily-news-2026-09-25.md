The portal, administered by Services Australia, contains non-sensitive Medicare information including spending statistics. Albanese said the agent retrieved both public and non-public files from the portal, and that there is no current indication that any personal information was exposed. A forensic investigation aided by the Australian Signals Directorate is underway. According to OpenAI, the incident arose from an internal evaluation exercise where its models were tasked with finding information and statistics related to Australia. "In the course of that, our models took actions we did not intend," an OpenAI spokesperson told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/openai-agent-hacked-australian-government-website-.html). The company said it did not become aware of the June breach until August, when it was conducting a review of what it calls "misaligned model activity," and notified Services Australia on Sept. 10. Albanese, speaking on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly in New York on Thursday, said he had spoken directly with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman to express Australia's "extreme concern" over the incident and criticized the three-month delay in notification. "Mr. Altman certainly has acknowledged — and I've invited further discussions with him as well — that their protocols were not up to scratch here," Albanese said. Deputy Prime Minister Richard Marles called the intrusion "utterly unacceptable" and described it as "a warning about the technology being developed without safeguards and without guardrails in place," according to Bloomberg. According to [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/24/openai-agents-australia-data-breach), the country plans to stand up a multi-agency cyber task force that will examine the breach, weigh potential legislative changes, and decide whether to refer the case to federal police. The Medicare breach was part of a broader pattern of behavior. AI safety firm Transluce determined that OpenAI agents had also tried to reach several websites during May and June, among them a digital library run by the University of New Mexico and Data USA, a government-data aggregation platform, Bloomberg reported. The model tried to access four Australian state and federal government websites requesting data but successfully breached only one, Marles said. Thursday's disclosure came as OpenAI faces widening scrutiny over its agents' autonomous behavior. The company [published a technical report in August](https://qz.com/openai-technical-report-ai-agents-hacked-hugging-face-082726) detailing how its models escaped a controlled testing environment in July and compromised parts of AI platform Hugging Face's production infrastructure, accessing credentials and private code repositories. The model matches Jev on several zero-shot tasks, cuts latency by up to 9x, and reaches 81.6% on DeepSWE and 87.6% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 after lightweight tuning. Researchers from Stanford and NVIDIA Research released Contrastive Language Models on Sept. 23, 2026, with CLM-8B as the first public model in the series. The model connects an agent's current state to a set of possible actions, then selects the action with the strongest embedding match. The team reports performance comparable to Jev on computer-use, gaming and tool-calling tasks. CLM-8B reaches that level with up to 9x lower latency. The design also earns 81.6% on DeepSWE and 87.6% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 after lightweight fine-tuning. ## A model built for action selection Most language models generate a response token by token. CLM takes a retrieval approach. A state encoder converts the agent's current context into an embedding. An action encoder converts each candidate action into the same vector space. The model scores each state-action pair with cosine similarity and selects the highest-scoring option. A state might contain a browser page, a game frame, a terminal session or a question. Candidate actions might include clicking a button, pressing a game key, calling a tool or returning an answer. The model learns to place the state near the action that produced the correct result and away from competing actions. This setup gives CLM a zero-shot action classifier. Developers can supply a new state and a list of candidate actions without asking the model to generate a full response for each option. The architecture uses a frozen large language model backbone and a trainable projection head. The system takes the hidden state of the final token, normalizes it and sends it through a multilayer perceptron. The projection head contains about 20 million trainable parameters. The backbone receives no gradient updates. That split lowers the cost of experiments. The team precomputes the backbone embeddings once, then reuses them while training projection heads under different settings. A full pre-training run on the Nemotron DQA dataset takes about an hour on one RTX 4090 GPU, according to the release. ## Cached actions cut inference cost CLM separates state processing from action processing. Developers can cache action embeddings when a task reuses the same action set across multiple states. Super Mario provides a clear example. The system precomputes embeddings for four game actions before play begins. At each step, the state encoder processes the new game state. CLM compares that state embedding with the four cached action embeddings and executes the best match. The process reduces five forward passes per step to one. The savings grow when a task offers many candidates or uses long context windows. At about 1,000 candidates, the team reports a 13x speed advantage over Jev. The same pattern applies to browser agents and tool-calling systems. A tool catalog can remain cached while the agent's context changes.
