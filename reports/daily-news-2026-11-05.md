The software supports a range of model servers, including vLLM and SGLang. **Cost pressure** The launch comes as businesses look for ways to cut the cost of serving AI models after the initial training phase. That project recorded a prefix-cache hit rate of about 98% and a blended serving cost of roughly USD $0.11 per million tokens. Caching matters because many AI workloads repeatedly draw on the same context, and reusing that information can reduce the fresh computation needed for each request. In another test, researchers used llm-d to deploy the open-weight GLM 5.2 model, described as having about 753 billion parameters, on NVIDIA GPUs already widely used in data centres. On benchmark agentic workloads with hundreds of parallel sessions, the system produced more than 6.6 million output tokens a minute at peak and served up to 3,000 concurrent coding agents without pre-emptions. According to figures released by the project, that setup would have delivered a per-token cost five to 10 times lower than equivalent commercial API pricing at current cloud rental rates.
