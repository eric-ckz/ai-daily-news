   - 摘要：# When AI agents agree on the data but not what it means Presented by SAP Enterprise AI agents that share the same data can still make conflicting decisions because raw data rarely captures the business context — the processes, policies and priorities that determine what that data means to the company. As agents take on more decision-making, those gaps in context become harder to contain. Three problems emerge when multiple agents act without shared business semantics: identical figures lead different functions to different conclusions, agents that work correctly on their own combine into the wrong outcome, and accuracy degrades as data accumulates. Enterprises have spent years loading ever more records into central data platforms, separating them in the process from the policies, processes and business context where they originated. That separation is at the root of the problem, says Irfan Khan, president and chief product officer for SAP Data & Analytics. “If you access data from a source environment and it doesn’t have the context, the likelihood is you’re going to make an ill-informed decision,” he explains. "Users learn from history only when institutional knowledge helps them separate signal from noise. An agent starts with no such knowledge unless the company supplies it." ## The agent coordination gap in multi-agent AI systems Traditional data strategies extract records from source systems and centralize them, which separates the data from the semantics and process context it carried at the source. The shift toward separating storage and compute let companies scale each independently and encouraged many to build multiple data lakes alongside on-premises estates, flat files and object stores, leading to fragmented data increasingly separated from its original business context. In multi-agent systems, missing context doesn’t stay a data problem. Once agents divide a business process among them, it becomes a coordination failure. For instance, a procurement agent negotiates longer payment terms to improve margins, a treasury agent delays payments to preserve liquidity, and a compliance agent holds payments until every validation clears. Each one is performing its assigned job correctly, but together they're stalling supplier payments long enough that critical vendors start deprioritizing the company, and no internal system flags anything as broken. Plus, few business processes run on a single agent from end to end. An order entry agent might hand off to one that checks inventory, which hands off to another that files a procurement request or decrements stock. If those steps run out of sequence, one agent can decrement an account before another has confirmed the goods exist, and the task can be completed, but the outcome can still be wrong. And there's no reason agents would coordinate any better than people do when nobody manages the handoffs, Khan says. “It’s almost like pushing out a task to 20 individual people to go and get it done for a client, and then you realize that nobody held a team meeting,” he says. “Nobody kept an inventory of where you were, or where you are within the process.” ## A business data fabric gives agents shared business context Giving agents access to distributed enterprise data solves only part of the problem. 6. **Unifying Data Context Is Essential to AI Agents' Success - Business Insider**
   - 摘要：By Manish Sood, founder & CEO of Reltio, an SAP company, and Irfan Khan, president & chief product officer of SAP Data & Analytics Sixty-three percent of organizations either lack or aren't sure they have the data management required for AI, according to Gartner. Worse, the firm predicts that companies will abandon 60% of AI projects by the end of this year due to a lack of AI-ready data. Not because of model performance, talent, or cost. It's the data they already have but can't use. Across every industry, enterprises are deploying autonomous AI agents to execute workflows and make decisions at machine speed. Yet the data those agents rely on remains fragmented, duplicated, and inconsistent. The agent isn't the bottleneck — the scattered data layer underneath it is. Before AI agents can deliver meaningful ROI, organizations must bridge the gap between content and context. Otherwise, they risk automating ambiguity at unprecedented speed. Ask a large organization how many active customers it has, and sales, finance, and support will each cite a different number. None of them is necessarily wrong.
