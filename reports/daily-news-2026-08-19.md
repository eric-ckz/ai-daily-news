The arrangement covers an initial 4.25 gigawatts at the Portsmouth campus, where OpenAI plans to build and operate an AI facility using NVIDIA GPUs, CPUs, networking and infrastructure software. NVIDIA may extend the deal to cover the remaining 3.75 gigawatts at the site. The move highlights how access to land, electricity and physical data centre space has become a constraint alongside chips, packaging, memory and networking as demand for large-scale AI computing grows. NVIDIA is applying the same supply-chain approach it has used for semiconductors to these infrastructure inputs. Under the structure it outlined, NVIDIA is supporting parts of the lease and power commitments for about 4 gigawatts over a 20-year term, with the site reserved for NVIDIA compute. The support does not cover the full cost of the campus or all of the tenant's obligations, and the guarantee is due to take effect in phases as data centres enter service between 2028 and 2030. OpenAI will pay the lease, while NVIDIA is using its scale and visibility into customer demand to help secure a location that can host multiple generations of its systems. As capacity comes online and lease payments are made, NVIDIA's remaining exposure would fall. **Site economics** NVIDIA described the Ohio site as a long-life asset that can be upgraded repeatedly as new generations of compute equipment are installed. Each generation of systems deployed at PORTS-Pike could amount to about 1.5 million NVIDIA GPUs and generate roughly USD $150 billion to USD $200 billion in revenue for NVIDIA. Over two decades, the campus could support several upgrade cycles. The emphasis is on securing a physical site with long-term access to power and real estate, while replacing the computing equipment inside it as newer systems become available. NVIDIA also set out the broader scale of its business with OpenAI, saying the company's existing and planned commitments represent about 12 gigawatts of NVIDIA compute through 2030, with scope to rise to about 16 gigawatts if the Ohio arrangement expands beyond the initial 4.25 gigawatts. At that level, the opportunity would represent around USD $600 billion of compute through 2030. The figures underline the scale of spending now being attached to a handful of large AI customers as the sector races to secure data centre capacity and electricity supply. **Selective approach** Most NVIDIA customers will continue to arrange their own land, power and shell capacity. That includes cloud service providers, enterprises and state-backed AI projects with the balance sheets and financing structures to sign long-term infrastructure contracts directly.
