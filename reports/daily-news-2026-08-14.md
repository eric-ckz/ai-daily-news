[NVIDIA](/tag/nvidia) has outlined an [800 VDC](/tag/800+vdc) (volt direct current) [power architecture](/tag/power+architecture) for [AI factories](/tag/ai+factories), aiming to support higher-density AI computing infrastructure. This architecture centres on distributing electricity through 800-volt direct current rather than conventional alternating current (AC) systems, which typically involve multiple conversion stages. The approach is reflected in NVIDIA's DSX reference designs, which are intended to guide operators from existing AC-based environments to hybrid systems and then to fully native 800 VDC facilities. This move comes as operators of large AI data centres seek methods to increase [rack density](/tag/rack+density) without overhauling entire sites. NVIDIA emphasised that the challenge extends beyond delivering power to a facility; it also involves efficiently moving that power from the grid to the compute equipment. ### Hybrid deployment route For existing sites, NVIDIA is preparing an MGX-compatible 800 VDC power rack, slated for availability in the second half of 2026. This unit is designed to integrate with current AC infrastructure, delivering 800 VDC to compute racks within the row without requiring modifications to a building's electrical system. The hybrid strategy targets operators who have already invested in land, power access, and physical infrastructure for AI facilities, enabling them to introduce higher-density compute within existing sites without a full rebuild. [Vladimir Troy](/tag/vladimir+troy), Vice President of [Data Centre Infrastructure](/tag/data+centre+infrastructure) at NVIDIA, stated that "800 VDC unlocks the compute performance and power density required for AI at scale." Troy added that NVIDIA is collaborating with over 80 ecosystem companies through the [Open Compute Project](/tag/open+compute+project) to provide a practical roadmap for AI factories. ### Industry collaboration and scaling NVIDIA is developing the 800 VDC architecture with [Google](/tag/google) and [Microsoft](/tag/microsoft) through the Open Compute Project. The three companies have jointly published a white paper and a low-voltage direct-current solid-state transformer specification through the group, establishing a common framework for equipment vendors and infrastructure providers. According to NVIDIA, more than 80 equipment manufacturers and infrastructure companies are already developing products to this specification.
