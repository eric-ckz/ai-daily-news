   - 摘要：[Skip to content](#content) # [Economist Writing Every Day](https://economistwritingeveryday.com/) ## [Paper Published on Persistent LLM Hallucinations](https://economistwritingeveryday.com/2026/10/17/paper-published-on-persistent-llm-hallucinations/) [Joy Buchanan](https://economistwritingeveryday.com/author/joybuchanan494/) I am pleased to announce the publication of “[LLM Hallucination of Citations in Economics Persists With Web-Enabled Models](https://doi.org/10.1002/soej.70069)” By Joy Buchanan, Stephen Hill, and Olga Shapoval > **ABSTRACT**: Despite significant advances since 2023 in large language model (LLM) capabilities and the introduction of web-enabled search functionality, citation hallucination remains a persistent problem in AI-generated academic content. We test GPT-4o, DeepSeek V3, web-enabled GPT-4o, and web-enabled GPT-5.1 across prompts derived from *Journal of Economic Literature* categories. Using a hierarchical citation validation framework, we find that even an advanced web-enabled model produces non-existent citations. While web-enabled models show improvement over their predecessors, the error rates remain high for scholarly applications. Our findings suggest that the fundamental challenge of citation hallucination persists despite technological advances, with implications for academic integrity and the reliable use of AI in research contexts. We provide an online tool for researchers to run LLMs through the benchmark test using our prompts. Suggested citation: Buchanan, J., Hill, S. and Shapoval, O. (2026), LLM Hallucination of Citations in Economics Persists With Web-Enabled Models. Southern Economic J. <https://doi.org/10.1002/soej.70069> After some time went by since we published [our 2024 paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/05694345231218454) on this topic and GPT became web-enabled, we thought that this silly problem with hallucinating up fake citations might have gone away. So, we went back to do our count again. We found that a significant number of references are still made up. I believe that this particular problem of fake citations will go away soon. However, I think the starkness of fake references illustrates something more general about the LLM technology and highlights the importance of verification. Feel free to cite our 2026 paper as evidence that hallucinations are still happening, despite improvements along many margins. Since we started down this track, OpenAI released a paper titled “Why language models hallucinate” at <https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/> Excerpt: > Hallucinations persist partly because current evaluation methods set the wrong incentives. While evaluations themselves do not directly cause hallucinations, most evaluations measure model performance in a way that encourages guessing rather than honesty about uncertainty. You can access our working paper of “LLM Hallucination of Citations in Economics Persists with Web-Enabled Models” on SSRN at <https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5318314> We are not in the slow-lane “LLMs are stochastic parrots” group.
