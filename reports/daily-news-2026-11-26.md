The two people said it appeared chip sellers were seeking to offload them because they were originally for use in data centers in which Chinese companies would rent access. They asked not to be named because they were discussing private information. The Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, announced an [interim final rule](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-commerce-china-lutnick.html) as part of a clampdown on Chinese companies bypassing export controls, which effectively ban the sale of the most advanced Nvidia chips to China, by accessing them remotely in overseas data centers. The rule came into effect on Nov. 19. Deals to find buyers for thousands of B300 GPUs, one of Nvidia's most advanced offerings, have been floated since the rule was announced, said Duhamel and one of the people. Nvidia declined to comment. BIS did not immediately respond to a request for comment. ## Scrutiny over Chinese access to Nvidia chips Nvidia has been facing increasing scrutiny from U.S. lawmakers over Chinese companies' access to its advanced chips. The chip giant warned its customers that the proposed Remote Access Security Act — which has passed the House of Representatives but is yet to pass the Senate — would give the U.S. government broader authority to control access to cloud services, potentially exposing both parties to "legal action," [sources previously told CNBC.](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-china-cloud-ai-chip-access.html) In July, the U.S. accused a Chinese AI lab, [Moonshot AI](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/moonshot-ai-nvidia-chips-europe-china.html), of accessing Nvidia chips via a facility in Thailand. CNBC reported on Nov. 5 that the company had [sought access to the chips](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/moonshot-ai-nvidia-chips-europe-china.html) via data centers in Europe. ![U.S. Trade Rep. Greer on new tariff proposals: Targeted tariffs in response to excess capacity](https://image.cnbcfm.com/api/v1/image/108559246-17956146901795614684-49853581115-1080pnbcnews.jpg?v=1795614689&w=750&h=422&vtcrop=y) U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Wednesday that the new rule announced last week was intended to close "loopholes." "For a long time, we have not allowed the sale of Blackwell chips, which are the highest AI chips or similar chips to China directly," he said. "Some rules were issued by the Commerce Department last week to close off indirect access, either smuggling through places like Southeast Asia or remote access from data centers in third countries." During Nvidia's third-quarter earnings call on Nov. 17, then-interim CEO Colette Kress said the company was still studying the controls, but expected performance to remain strong because demand for chips far exceeded supply.
