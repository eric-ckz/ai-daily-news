4. **AI agents vs physical AI: How software automation differs from embodied robotics**
   - 摘要：Today’s Artificial intelligence has evolved far beyond simple chatbots that merely answer questions or generate text. Two major branches of autonomous AI now dominate engineering and enterprise roadmaps. AI agents and physical AI. While both are built to pursue goals independently rather than waiting for step-by-step human prompts, they operate in fundamentally different environments. One moves bits across software networks, while the other moves atoms in the real world. Understanding where AI agents end and physical AI begins is essential for engineers, developers, and businesses deciding how to automate digital workflows versus physical operations. ## How AI agents automate digital workflows AI agents, often referred to as agentic AI, are autonomous software systems designed to plan, reason, and execute multi-step tasks inside digital environments. According to IMEC and enterprise software architectures, standard generative models simply respond to a prompt and leave the human user to act on the output. An AI agent takes an overarching objective, breaks it into logical subtasks, accesses external software tools or application programming interfaces (APIs), and carries out the workflow end to end. Under the hood, AI agents typically pair large language models (LLMs) or reasoning models with memory modules, orchestration frameworks, and standardized connectors such as the Model Context Protocol (MCP). ### More from AI and Robotics See All When tasked with resolving a supply-chain delay or debugging a software repository, an AI agent can query databases, write and test code, update enterprise resource planning (ERP) records, and send notifications without continuous human hand-holding. Its inputs are text, code, structured data, and digital signals; its outputs are API calls, file edits, and software transactions. ## How physical AI interacts with the real world Physical AI extends autonomous intelligence out of the cloud and into embodied machines, including humanoid robots, autonomous vehicles, warehouse mobile manipulators, and industrial drones. Rather than processing static text or database rows, physical AI systems must perceive, predict, and act upon continuous, messy real-world physics such as gravity, friction, inertia, and shifting lighting. To operate safely alongside humans, physical AI combines multimodal sensor arrays, cameras, LiDAR, radar, force-torque sensors, and accelerometers with specialized models that map sensor data directly to motor commands. In a 2025 arXiv paper introducing the Cosmos World Foundation Models, NVIDIA researchers detailed how video- and physics-aware foundation models help robots internalize real-world dynamics in simulation before deploying to physical hardware. Unlike digital agents that output text or software commands, physical AI outputs real-time actuator torques, joint trajectories, and navigation paths, often running directly on edge processors to avoid network latency. ## Key engineering differences and failure stakes The clearest distinction between AI agents and physical AI lies in their operating constraints and the cost of errors. An AI agent runs inside deterministic digital infrastructure where actions can often be rolled back, sandboxed, or audited in logs if a model hallucinates or misinterprets an instruction. Physical AI enjoys no such undo button. If a vision-language-action model misjudges the weight of a fragile component or fails to detect a wet factory floor, the result can be a dropped payload, damaged machinery, or injury to nearby workers.
