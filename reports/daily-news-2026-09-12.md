The company, based in Reno, Nevada, is pursuing a contrarian technical strategy: using commodity LPDDR5X memory instead of the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) that dominates Nvidia's data center GPUs. Positron argues that HBM's theoretical peak bandwidth rarely translates into real-world performance on inference workloads, where random memory access patterns undermine utilization rates. Nvidia GPUs typically achieve less than 30% of their theoretical memory bandwidth on transformer inference workloads, according to Positron's analysis. The startup claims its Asimov chip achieves more than 90% utilization on the same tasks. That efficiency gap, the company argues, allows cheaper memory to deliver competitive effective throughput without the supply chain bottlenecks that constrain HBM production. HBM requires advanced CoWoS packaging and consumes scarce production capacity from suppliers like SK hynix and Samsung. By designing around LPDDR5X, which is available from multiple vendors and uses standard organic substrates, Positron is attempting to sidestep one of the most constrained segments of the AI hardware supply chain. The company's existing Atlas system is already deployed in more than 50 racks at Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, with customers including Parasail, Jump Trading, and i3d.net.
