The shift has prompted some investors to compare the chip giant to a central bank for the AI economy. “Nvidia is kind of becoming the central bank of AI, the Federal Reserve of AI,” **Gavin Baker**, founder of **Atreides Management**, said during a discussion on the *All-In* podcast. At the center of the strategy is a financing model that could allow AI infrastructure companies to acquire expensive GPU systems without paying the entire cost upfront. Under the model described on the podcast, buyers can finance Nvidia-powered systems and repay lenders using revenue generated by renting computing capacity or providing AI inference services. Nvidia can help connect those buyers with private capital while providing assurances around the future economic value of its GPUs. Residual-value guarantees covering what the hardware could potentially earn several years later could make it easier for banks and investment firms to finance GPU infrastructure. The concept resembles financing structures already common for assets such as commercial aircraft, where lenders underwrite equipment partly based on its expected future value and cash-generating ability. #### Wall Street Moves Deeper Into AI Infrastructure Large financial institutions and alternative asset managers, including Goldman Sachs, KKR and Blackstone, could provide much of the underlying capital and underwriting. Nvidia, meanwhile, would play a central role in establishing the economics surrounding the hardware, including residual values and potentially revenue-sharing arrangements. **David Sacks**, venture capitalist and co-host of ***All-In***, described the strategy as a way of “removing the finance constraint” on AI infrastructure development. That could become increasingly important as AI companies, cloud providers and specialized data-center operators race to deploy enormous amounts of computing capacity. Rather than limiting expansion to what hyperscalers and other operators can fund directly from their balance sheets, financing structures backed by expected GPU-generated cash flow could bring much larger pools of private capital into the market. #### Could GPUs Become a New Asset Class? Baker rejected the idea that the approach amounts to circular financing in which a supplier effectively funds customers to purchase its own products. Instead, he characterized the structure as asset-backed lending supported by anticipated revenue from computing infrastructure. The longer-term possibility is that standardized GPU systems and their associated cash flows could eventually be packaged into securities, potentially creating a new institutional asset class around AI infrastructure. That would represent another major evolution for Nvidia, transforming the company from primarily a semiconductor supplier into an influential participant in the financial architecture supporting the AI industry. There are risks. The biggest would be a significant oversupply of computing capacity. If developers build data centers based on assumptions of persistently high prices for AI compute and those prices fall sharply, the economics supporting GPU-backed financing could weaken. The comparison is the telecommunications industry’s “dark fiber” problem following the dot-com boom, when enormous amounts of network capacity were built ahead of sustainable demand.
