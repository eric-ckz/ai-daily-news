The deal values the four-year-old startup at about $100 million, including the new funding. Different workloads demand distinct trade-offs between speed, cost, and performance. Deploying and operating AI models to meet business requirements often also requires complex adjustments. Simplismart’s software sits between AI models and the computing infrastructure they run on and adjusts how models are deployed and managed to meet customers’ requirements. The Bengaluru-based startup offers companies a way to deploy and run AI models through its own platform or within their existing cloud and on-premises infrastructure. Alongside its investment, Nvidia has been working with Simplismart on technical integrations, including around its Dynamo inference software, Jain told TechCrunch. The collaboration involves Simplismart’s engineers and Nvidia’s solutions architects, while Nvidia has also helped introduce the startup to potential customers and partners, Jain stated.    - 摘要：UPDATED 15:11 EDT / OCTOBER 27 2026 ### 11 insights from the CASPA CEO Summit: AI infrastructure hardware becomes a systems game Faster chips alone no longer define the AI buildout. Hundreds of thousands of processors now work together as one machine, so AI infrastructure hardware is designed and judged as a complete system. That system runs from the optical links between racks to the memory, storage and power that keep GPUs busy. The next phase will be defined by the bottlenecks around the processor. Copper is giving way to light, and the memory wall is moving rather than falling. Power systems built on cycles of up to a decade must keep pace with chips that turn over every year. Demand is not in doubt: Memory and optical laser capacity are booked out for years. “A lot of the focus in AI has been very much on compute and on memory, but our view is actually AI is a data system,” said Irving Tan (pictured), chief executive officer of Western Digital Corp. “Ultimately, you need the entire ecosystem to work together and to be able to move in lockstep.” Tan and other industry experts spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier during The CEO Summit in Santa Clara, California. The inaugural summit marked the 35th anniversary of the Chinese American Semiconductor Professional Association. The interviews covered optical interconnects, inference economics, memory pooling, maskless lithography, physics-based computing, power resilience and AI-driven chip design. Here are 11 standout insights from the event: ### 1. Hard drives gain throughput as storage becomes the AI data layer. Data creates intelligence and storage makes it persist, so data must be split into hot, warm and cold tiers across the cloud, the edge and devices, Tan explained. Citing IDC, he noted that the amount of data generated is projected to triple to more than 700 zettabytes over three years. The step beyond today’s rack-scale systems is racks tailored to whether a workload is latency-critical, throughput-hungry or energy-constrained. Maskless e-beam lithography targets the shift from Moore’s Law to advanced packaging. Multibeam shrank an electron-beam column from 80 centimeters tall to the size of a marking pen, so arrays of columns can write a wafer in parallel.
