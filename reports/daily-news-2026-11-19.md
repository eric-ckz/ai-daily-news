Log in](/login) # LLM Report Audit: 22 of 40 Copy Benchmark Scores, 1 Discloses Training Data in Full *Found first: a primary source the press has not covered yet.* Bahaeddin Türkoğlu audited 40 LLM technical reports and found that 22 took some or all benchmark comparison scores from other papers rather than re-running them, and only 1 disclosed data mixture, token count, and compute together. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20003) also examined architecture code for 48 models added to Hugging Face transformers in 2025 and found 39 share at least two of their three core building blocks with an earlier model, most often Llama. ## What the source says Türkoğlu drew a random sample of 40 technical reports and analyzed novelty claims and reproducibility disclosures, with two independent coders. Of 73 architectural or training novelty claims, 19 included a controlled ablation, and only 4 reports described more than one training run. Eight ran comparison models under a single evaluation setup, and 8 included any third-party evaluation. The author notes that an untested claim may still be true, and that copied scores are sometimes unavoidable when a comparison model cannot be re-run. The paper proposes seven minimum reporting rules and releases code and data publicly. ## Why it matters Capability rankings from LLM technical reports shape which models practitioners adopt and fund. When 22 of 40 reports use benchmark scores drawn from different evaluation setups, comparisons across papers are not grounded in a shared baseline. The architectural analysis adds a separate issue: 39 of 48 models in the Hugging Face library share the same core structure as earlier ones, yet the reports rarely test which parts are new.    - 摘要：# Nvidia says it will stop selling some AI data center chips to China Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress said Tuesday that the company has stopped shipping artificial intelligence chips from its Hopper series to China, despite earlier U.S. approval to sell the technology there. The Hopper line, designed to speed training of AI models, includes the powerful H200 chip, which the U.S. Department of Commerce approved for sale in China in January. Kress didn't provide a reason for the decision, but the strategic shift comes amid heightened scrutiny of Nvidia's sales in China following revelations that former CEO and cofounder Jensen Huang had a relationship with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Huang, who successfully lobbied the Trump administration to ease export controls on its advanced chips, had agreed to give the U.S. government 15% of revenue generated by sales of its AI chips in China. Kress said that sales of licensed Hopper products to China totaled $2.4 billion in the third quarter, or about 2% of its total revenue. But "our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China," she said in a call with Wall Street analysts to discuss Nvidia's latest financial results. Dilin Wu, a research strategist at Pepperstone, told Agence France-Presse that "the timing says quite a lot." "We don't know exactly what prompted the decision to stop shipments, but it certainly looks like Nvidia is trying to draw a clearer line around its China business and reassure regulators that compliance comes first," Wu said. Nvidia also said on Tuesday that its board has formed a panel to oversee its national security policies and compliance with U.S. export controls. Washington still bans exports of Nvidia's more advanced Blackwell and Rubin GPUs to China, citing security concerns over AI's use in military applications. Nvidia reported robust growth as Kress hosted her first earnings call since Huang resigned last week after it emerged that he had at least extramarital affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman who U.S. authorities have linked to Chinese intelligence and a direct subordinate at Nvidia. The chipmaker reported fiscal third-quarter profits of $65.3 billion, topping analyst forecasts and up 105% from earnings of $31.9 billion in the year-ago period. Nvidia's revenue rose to $115.8 billion, compared with $57 billion a year ago.
