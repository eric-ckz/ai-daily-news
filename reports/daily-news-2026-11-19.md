A generative AI assistant can understand the request, explain refund eligibility and draft a response.An AI agent can go further. It can retrieve the booking, check the applicable policy, verify the passenger's details, initiate the authorised refund process and update the relevant records.The distinction is between providing information and acting on it. This does not mean that an AI agent has independent intentions or unrestricted authority. It then accesses approved company records, checks inventory levels and examines available logistics updates.After gathering information, it may identify a transportation delay, contact the supplier through an authorised channel and examine whether alternative inventory is available.It can then prepare a revised delivery plan and inform the procurement manager. Placing an alternative order, particularly one involving additional expenditure, may require human approval.The process involves several connected capabilities:Understanding the request: The agent interprets the instruction and identifies the intended outcome.Planning: It divides the objective into smaller tasks and determines their sequence.Accessing information: It retrieves relevant details from databases, documents and business applications.Taking action: It uses authorised tools to update records, send messages or initiate transactions.Checking the outcome: It examines the results and adjusts its next steps or transfers control to a human.Unlike a fixed sequence of automated instructions, an agent can adapt its next action to information gathered during the task.
