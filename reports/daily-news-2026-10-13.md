8. **[Open Source AI] QGIS Bans AI-Generated Contributions After Maintainer Burnout | LavX News**
   - 摘要：[Skip to content](#main-content) [News](/) English #AI # QGIS Bans AI-Generated Contributions After Maintainer Burnout • ![Elena Varga](/_next/image?url=%2Fauthors%2Felena-varga.webp&w=64&q=75) [Elena Varga](/author/elena-varga) •2 min read Share this article The QGIS project has adopted a strict policy prohibiting AI-generated code and content contributions, reversing its previous 'human in the loop' approach after maintainers reported unsustainable review burdens and erosion of community trust. The QGIS project has formally banned all AI-generated contributions, marking a sharp reversal from its earlier policy that permitted AI-assisted work with human oversight. The change, enacted through QGIS Enhancement Proposal 428 (QEP428), passed a community vote this week and takes effect immediately. Core developer Nyall Dawson proposed the ban after maintainers faced what they described as an unmanageable flood of AI-generated pull requests. The previous policy, QEP408, required contributors to tag AI-assisted work and maintain a "human in the loop." In practice, the tagging system failed — roughly 50 PRs carried the AI-assisted label, and about 40 were closed without merging. Others slipped through untagged. "AI use from external contributors puts a massive and unbearable pressure on the shoulders of our maintainers, who already had a tough job before the era of LLMs," wrote QGIS PSC member Julien Cabieces (haubourg) in the discussion. "AI stream is a lot higher than what human review can take seriously, leading to either a longer list of un-reviewed PRs or not fully reviewed code and quality issues." The project lost at least one high-value contributor due to the strain, according to Dawson. The new policy adopts a "closed door" approach: all AI-generated contributions are prohibited, with the possibility of narrowly reopening specific use cases — such as IDE autocomplete or language translation — only after careful case-by-case discussion. Contributor Amalvir, who had submitted AI-assisted bug fixes, supported the ban: "By doing this, we've broken a trust contract between the reviewer and the contributor. The contract that, to contribute at such a big project, you know what you're doing." Not all feedback was unanimous. Developer Kannes argued the policy's broad wording — "assisted" and "used" — could inadvertently block legitimate uses like AI-assisted code review, security auditing, or learning unfamiliar codebases. Dawson acknowledged the risk: "Yes, we are aware we might throw the baby with the bathwater, but the baby might be non-viable." The policy's enforcement relies on contributor honesty. As PSC chair Tim Sutton (timlinux) put it: "The measure of compliance is 'did you in good conscience write the code yourself' — we are not set up to peer into your computer." The ban extends beyond code to documentation, bug reports, discussions, PR descriptions, and all written interactions on GitHub. The QEP repository itself is also covered. QGIS joins a growing list of open-source projects restricting AI contributions, including the Linux kernel, which in 2024 warned maintainers about AI-generated patches that wasted reviewer time. The difference: QGIS has made the prohibition explicit policy rather than informal guidance. The project plans to revisit specific exemptions at its upcoming user conference. For now, the door is shut.
