The San Jose-based company is developing a central processing unit (CPU) designed for data centers, targeting growing demand for computing infrastructure supporting artificial intelligence applications. Nuvacore has yet to release a product, and its latest funding round remains open, with the final valuation and amount raised subject to change. The Information previously reported that the startup was seeking at least $200 million, but the proposed $2.5 billion valuation had not been disclosed. Founded by semiconductor engineers Gerard Williams, John Bruno and Ram Srinivasan, Nuvacore is pursuing an unconventional chip development strategy. The company plans to design its processor's core functionality before selecting an architecture, potentially avoiding limitations associated with Intel and Advanced Micro Devices' x86 technology or Arm-based designs. Williams previously worked at Apple and founded Nuvia, which Qualcomm acquired for $1.4 billion in 2021. Demand for data center CPUs has accelerated as AI systems require processors to coordinate specialized chips and operate autonomous AI agents. Intel and AMD have historically dominated the market, although Arm-based processors have gained traction through custom designs used by Amazon and Microsoft. Nvidia has also entered the market with its Vera CPU, which CEO Jensen Huang expects to generate $20 billion in sales during the company's fiscal year ending in January. Investor interest in semiconductor startups has strengthened alongside rising AI infrastructure spending. Semiconductor startups attracted approximately $10.7 billion in investment during the first five months of 2026, compared with $12.2 billion raised globally throughout 2025, according to Crunchbase.
