In a separate trend uncovered through its own threat hunting, CERT-In observed cybercriminals using publicly available AI-native offensive-security frameworks to conduct reconnaissance, exploit security weaknesses and carry out a financially motivated campaign against Indian entities. 7. **DCF Global: The Nordics' AI Boom Meets the Power Test | Data Center Frontier**
   - 摘要：Finland's grid operator reports nearly 5 GW of eventual data center capacity associated with signed connection agreements, underscoring the scale of the country's AI infrastructure ambitions. JLL, CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield and datacenterHawk research points to growing Nordic investment appeal, alongside significant questions about power availability, preleasing and project execution. The region's experience offers an increasingly relevant comparison with North America, where power delivery, infrastructure costs and community acceptance are similarly reshaping data center development. The Nordic countries have long occupied a distinctive position in the global data center industry. Their appeal rests on a familiar combination of cool climates, comparatively abundant low-carbon electricity, political stability, and sophisticated energy and communications infrastructure. Now, the accelerating demand for AI computing is transforming that established proposition into something considerably larger. Across Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Iceland, hyperscalers, AI cloud providers, infrastructure developers and institutional investors are advancing a new generation of high-density data center projects. Their ambitions increasingly extend beyond conventional cloud regions and enterprise colocation into multihundred-megawatt AI campuses and integrated energy infrastructure. The scale of that transformation is particularly evident in Finland, where the country's transmission system operator, Fingrid, reported in August that data center projects with signed grid-connection agreements represented nearly 5 gigawatts of planned eventual capacity. That figure encompasses projects connected to transmission and distribution networks. It does not represent operating data center capacity, nor does a connection agreement guarantee that a project will be completed at its proposed size. Nevertheless, it illustrates the extraordinary volume of investment now being directed toward a country that, until recently, occupied a secondary position behind Sweden and Norway in the Nordic data center market. The acceleration comes with a paradox. The electricity resources, industrial sites and favorable environmental conditions attracting AI developers are also becoming subjects of increasingly consequential public-policy decisions. Finland has raised electricity taxes on data centers and is considering changes to grid-connection priorities. Denmark has already introduced a system that places most large data center projects in a lower-priority category for constrained grid capacity. Meanwhile, investment continues to accelerate. For the Nordics, the central question is no longer whether the region can attract AI infrastructure development. It is how much of the announced capacity can be financed, permitted, connected and ultimately brought into production without compromising the energy-system advantages and sustainability commitments on which the market's appeal depends. The strongest evidence of Finland's changing position came in September, when Google announced plans to invest €13 billion in Finnish digital infrastructure and related projects over 2027 and 2028. The investment encompasses operations and planned development in Hamina, Muhos, Vaala and Kajaani, extending Google's presence beyond its established Hamina campus. At the same time, Google and Finnish energy company Fortum announced a 22-year nuclear power purchase agreement covering up to 50% of the generating capacity at the Loviisa nuclear power plant. The arrangement provides financial support for extending the plant's operating life through 2050, while the companies have also agreed to explore additional nuclear generation, renewable energy and grid-flexibility solutions.
