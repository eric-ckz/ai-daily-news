4. **AI Agent Security Needs More Than Least Privilege**
   - 摘要：* [Blog](https://petri.com/blog/ "View all posts") * [Identity Management](https://petri.com/category/identity-management/ "View Identity Management articles") * Post * + [Identity Management](https://petri.com/category/identity-management/ "Article posted in Identity Management") + [Artificial Intelligence](https://petri.com/category/artificial-intelligence/ "Article posted in Artificial Intelligence") * Nov 09, 2026 # AI Agent Security Needs More Than Least Privilege Least privilege limits what an AI agent can do, but not whether it behaves as expected. Without behavioral visibility, an agent with valid credentials could act across your environment at machine speed and nothing would flag it as unusual. [By Russell Smith on Nov 09, 2026](https://petri.com/author/russell-smith/ "View all articles by Russell Smith") ![copilot studio agents hero approved](https://petri.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/copilot-studio-agents-hero-approved.png) AI agent security is creating a familiar problem for IT teams. Enterprises are giving autonomous agents access to applications, infrastructure, and sensitive data, sometimes with the same excessive privileges organizations have spent decades trying to eliminate from human accounts. The difference is speed. An overprivileged human identity presents a security risk and an overprivileged AI agent can potentially act across an environment at machine speed. *“These are identities, these are entities,”* said Heath Mullins, Chief Evangelist at [ExtraHop](https://www.extrahop.com/) and a former [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/) analyst. *“They should be assigned appropriate permissions to accomplish the task at hand. And that task should not be overly broad.”* But least privilege only answers one part of the AI security problem. Organizations also need to know whether agents behave as expected after they receive access. ## AI agent security starts with least privilege The [principle of least privilege](https://identitymanagementinstitute.org/the-principle-of-least-privilege/) isn’t new. Organizations have spent years trying to stop administrators, applications, and service accounts from receiving unnecessary permissions. Mullins argues that enterprises should apply the same thinking to AI agents. Some organizations, he said, are giving agents broad administrative permissions to find vulnerabilities, recommend remediation, and identify systems requiring patches. That sounds attractive because an agent can automate work across an enterprise.
