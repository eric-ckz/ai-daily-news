In an [agentic wallet](https://www.ledger.com/academy/glossary/agentic-wallet), an [AI agent](https://www.ledger.com/academy/glossary/ai-agents) watches markets around the clock and prepares transactions, payments, and routine portfolio tasks within parameters you set. AI is already acting on your behalf. The question is whether you control what it does. Ledger Agent Intent is built to give you that control, and it is one layer of the Ledger Agent Stack described in [Ledger’s 2026 AI Security Roadmap](https://www.ledger.com/blog-2026-ai-security-roadmap). To be useful, an agent needs access to the things it acts on, such as email, files, credentials, and wallets, and that same access makes mistakes and manipulation costly. The Agent Stack answers with one principle. Agents propose. You approve. Your Ledger signer enforces. ## What is Ledger Agent Intent? Think of each suggestion from Ledger Agent Intent as a proposal, not a transaction. When operating within the policy that you set, your agent can propose transactions that align with your overall goals, such as spending limits or specific transaction types. Each proposal is designed to require physical confirmation on your Ledger signer, the device that keeps your private keys offline, before the transaction is broadcast to the blockchain. You still have to review the proposal, and the transaction is designed not to move forward until you do. The proposal appears in the Ledger Agent Intent interface. Confirming it happens on your Ledger signer itself, the same action required for any transaction. Ledger Agent Intent gives every request for your approval one place to land. Your agents keep working on everything else, and each proposal waits in your queue until you are ready to review it. If you run several agents, their requests arrive in the same queue. Your AI agent can watch for the conditions you care about and prepare the matching action, whether that is a swap when a price you chose is reached, a staking position that needs rebalancing, or funds that are ready to collect. It can be as aggressive or reserved as you prompt it. It isn’t realistic to sit in front of a computer all hours of the day, waiting for an opportunity to align with your goals, so you don’t have to. Your agent waits until an opportunity or task comes up, and then proposes the transaction on your behalf. All you have to do is decide if you want to confirm it.
