8. **[Infra & Chips] Intel GPU D-series AI inference chip specs revealed at OCP 2026**
   - 摘要：[![Quartz](https://static.qz.com/quartz-v1.137.0/_next/static/media/quartz-logo.1poxn91047rb6.svg?dpl=1nln7blm5540)](/) [A.I.](/tech-innovation/ai) # Intel is revealing specs for its new GPU D-series AI inference chip at industry summit The chip, formerly code-named Crescent Island, delivers 1.3 petaflops of FP8 compute and 480GB of memory within a 350-watt power envelope [By Cris Tolomia](/author/cristolomia)·2 min read·Updated October 13, 2026 [Add QZ to Google](https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=qz.com) Justin Sullivan / Getty Images Intel[$INTC](/quote/INTC) [shared technical details](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/artificial-intelligence/intel-accelerates-open-infrastructure-agentic-ai-era.html) Monday on its GPU D-series AI inference chip at the Open Compute Project Global Summit, giving the product formerly code-named Crescent Island its official name and disclosing key performance specifications for the first time. According to Intel, the chip is based on the Xe3P architecture and delivers up to 1.3 petaflops of FP8 compute alongside 1.5 TB/s of memory bandwidth and as much as 480GB of LPDDR5X memory, all within a 350-watt power envelope. It uses a standard PCIe form factor and air-cooled design, allowing deployment in existing data center environments without specialized rack or cooling infrastructure. Intelsaid the GPU D-series is designed for large language models, long-context workloads and concurrent inference services. A single card can support models with up to 300 billion parameters, while an eight-GPU system can handle models exceeding one trillion parameters, according to the company. "Inference is becoming the defining AI workload, and enterprises need infrastructure that delivers the right performance at the right cost," Anil Nanduri, vice president of AI Products and GTM at Intel's Data Center Group, said in a statement. "With IntelGPU D-series, we're focused on making high-capacity inference practical to deploy and economical to scale." Customer sampling of GPU D-series products is underway, Intelsaid, though the company did not announce a broad launch date or pricing. The GPU D-series targets a different performance and infrastructure profile than Nvidia[$NVDA](/quote/NVDA)'s highest-end hardware, according to [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/intels-crescent-island-ai-accelerator-officially-powers-new-gpu-d-series-products-chip-delivers-1-3-peak-pflops-of-fp8-compute-and-1-5-tb-s-of-memory-bandwidth). Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack-scale system packs 72 Rubin GPUs, each capable of 17.5 petaflops of FP8 throughput, but the platform demands liquid cooling and draws substantially more power, according to Tom's Hardware. Intelalso introduced new Xeon-based rack designs at the summit. Select Xeon 6+ configurations can scale beyond 46,000 cores per rack at 133.45 kilowatts of total rack power, the company said. Many of the new rack designs use the Open Compute Project's Open Rack v3 standard to simplify deployment in open data center infrastructure. Intelsaid its commercialization efforts involve a range of partners across the supply chain, including OEMs, ODMs, neoclouds, independent software vendors, appliance providers, and AI-native companies. The company also highlighted a contribution to the OCP's Open Adapter Card workgroup, which is working to define a standardized sub-rack accelerator card form factor to improve interoperability across system designs.
