[VAST Data](/tag/vast+data) has launched [DataEnclave](/tag/dataenclave), a [confidential AI](/tag/confidential+ai) system designed to enable organisations to run AI models securely on sensitive corporate data. Built on [NVIDIA](/tag/nvidia) confidential computing technology, the solution allows deployment within private data centres or trusted cloud environments, preventing exposure of customer data or model weights to infrastructure operators. This addresses critical privacy and sovereignty challenges for regulated industries. The system is specifically engineered to tackle a common issue in sectors like financial services, healthcare, and government, where data cannot be moved to external AI services and model owners are hesitant to place their software in untrusted environments. DataEnclave extends VAST's AI Operating System and DataEngine software with a hardware-isolated runtime and cryptographic attestation. This ensures that the environment and its policy are verified before encrypted models and data are released for processing within a secure enclave, where they remain protected in CPU and GPU memory. NVIDIA's role is central, leveraging confidential computing capabilities across its Hopper, Blackwell, and Rubin platforms. VAST Data's software utilises these to establish secure container runtimes through CPU and GPU trusted execution environments, safeguarding guest memory, GPU memory, and traffic over NVLink connections. The system also includes attestation checks before decryption keys are released, supports separate key management for enterprises and model providers, and stores audit logs in VAST's database layer. Customers retain control of their data keys, while model builders manage model keys and weights, with administrators and infrastructure operators unable to access either during workloads. The secure runtime can also isolate AI agents and govern their access to data, systems, and tools. The offering targets industries with stringent privacy, sovereignty, and internal control requirements, including sovereign and air-gapped deployments. The demand for secure, sovereign AI compute is increasing in the Asia-Pacific region, including potentially India, making solutions like DataEnclave relevant for local data sovereignty and privacy needs. Indian organisations in regulated sectors, such as financial services and government, could leverage this technology to deploy AI models on sensitive data while adhering to local [data protection](/tag/data+protection) regulations. The partnership with NVIDIA, a key player in India's AI infrastructure push, indicates potential for this technology to be adopted in Indian data centres and AI factories.
